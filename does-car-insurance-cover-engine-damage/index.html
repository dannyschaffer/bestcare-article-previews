Does car insurance cover engine damage?

Car insurance covers engine damage caused by something that happened to the car. It doesn’t cover an engine that simply failed. That line decides most engine claims in South Africa.

Quick answer Comprehensive car insurance covers engine damage only when an insured event causes it, such as a collision, fire, theft or attempted theft. It doesn’t cover mechanical or electrical breakdown, wear and tear, or damage caused by poor maintenance. Those belong to warranties and maintenance plans. Warranty and mechanical breakdown disputes made up 606 of the 12,188 complaints lodged with the short-term insurance ombud in 2023 (Moonstone, 2024).

When car insurance covers engine damage, and when it doesn’t

Car insurance pays for sudden loss or damage caused by an insured peril. It doesn’t pay for a part that wears out or fails on its own. Most South African motor policies contain a clause along the lines of “we will not compensate you for loss or damage arising from mechanical, electrical or electronic breakdown or defect”, alongside exclusions for wear and tear and gradual deterioration (Arrive Alive, 2022).

“Mechanical breakdowns or vehicle repairs are typically covered under a warranty or motor plan, which is separate and different to a motor insurance policy.”— Nazeer Hoosen, then Executive Director, PPS Short-Term Insurance, speaking to FAnews (2013)

He went on to note that a mechanical breakdown caused by an accident or insured peril is covered. That distinction still holds today. The question is always what caused the damage, not which part was damaged. The National Financial Ombud’s non-life tips make the same point from the other side: keep up with maintenance to avoid a rejection on the grounds of wear and tear and lack of maintenance.

Two ombud cases show how the grey areas get resolved, and why reading the exact clause matters more than the headline “comprehensive”.

What the ombud has decided in engine claims

In both cases below, the insurer’s rejection was upheld because the policy wording tied engine cover to a specific cause. They were decided by the former Ombudsman for Short-Term Insurance, whose work is now done by the National Financial Ombud.

The flooded road and the bent conrods

A driver went through water on a flooded road. The car stopped and wouldn’t restart. The insurer’s assessor found a wet air filter, an intercooler full of water and conrods bent by hydraulic lock. The policy only covered engine damage that happened “upon impact” during an accident. The ombud first leaned towards payment, but on review agreed that the seizure didn’t happen on impact and upheld the rejection (FAnews, 2021).

The snapped conrod and the engine fire

A conrod snapped while the car was being driven. Oil leaked onto the hot exhaust and the car caught fire. Fire is normally an insured event, but the ombud upheld the insurer’s view that the fire flowed directly from an excluded mechanical failure (Arrive Alive, 2022).

If you get caught in rising water, don’t try to restart a stalled engine. Get to safety, then call your insurer or roadside assistance. The same applies to wrong fuel: MiWay’s advice is not to turn the ignition on at all (MiWay, 2026).

Where breakdown cover actually comes from

If your worry is an engine that fails on its own, the protection sits outside your insurance policy. There are four main places to look:

  • Manufacturer’s warranty on newer vehicles, subject to the service schedule being kept.
  • Service or maintenance plans, which pay for scheduled servicing and, for maintenance plans, certain wear-related parts.
  • Mechanical breakdown warranty products, sold separately, which pay towards repairs after a mechanical or electrical failure, within their own limits and exclusions.
  • The Consumer Protection Act. If you bought the car from a dealer, section 56 gives an implied six-month warranty of quality. The Motor Industry Ombudsman of South Africa deals with these disputes, and its published case studies include a dealer being required to repair a used vehicle whose engine failed shortly after sale.

Good records help on every one of these routes. Service invoices, oil-change receipts and warning-light history are exactly what a warranty provider, dealer or insurer will ask for.

If your engine claim is rejected

Ask for the reason and the assessor’s findings in writing, then challenge the decision with evidence if you believe an insured event caused the damage. An independent mechanical report on the cause, together with your service history, is usually the strongest evidence you’ll have. Key takeaways

  • Car insurance covers engine damage caused by an insured event, not an engine that fails on its own.
  • Mechanical and electrical breakdown, wear and tear and poor maintenance are standard exclusions.
  • Flood water, potholes and wrong fuel depend heavily on the exact wording.
  • Damage that flows from an excluded breakdown, even a fire, can remain excluded.
  • Breakdown protection comes from warranties, maintenance plans, breakdown products and the CPA.
  • Keep service records. They support both warranty claims and insurance disputes.

Engine damage and car insurance FAQs

General answers for South African motor policies. Your schedule and wording decide the outcome of any claim.

Does comprehensive car insurance cover engine damage in South Africa?

Only when the damage is caused by an insured event, such as a collision, fire, theft or attempted theft, or another peril the policy covers. Engine failure from mechanical or electrical breakdown, wear and tear or poor maintenance is a standard exclusion in South African motor policies.

Is a blown or seized engine covered by car insurance?

Usually not, if it seized because of lack of oil, overheating from a failed part, a snapped timing belt or general wear. Those are mechanical breakdown or maintenance issues. If a collision caused the damage that led to the seizure, the accident damage may be covered, subject to the wording.

Will insurance pay if I drive through a flooded road and water damages the engine?

It depends on your wording. In one case, the short-term insurance ombud upheld an insurer’s rejection of an engine-only hydraulic-lock claim because the policy only covered engine damage that happened on impact during an accident. Other wordings may treat it differently, so check the clause before you need it.

What covers mechanical breakdown if insurance doesn’t?

The manufacturer’s warranty, a service or maintenance plan, or a separate mechanical breakdown warranty product. If you bought the car from a dealer, the Consumer Protection Act also gives you an implied six-month warranty of quality on goods, including used vehicles, subject to conditions.

Does car insurance cover putting the wrong fuel in my car?

Many policies don’t cover engine damage caused by misfuelling, and some cover it only as an optional extra or under specific conditions. Don’t start the engine once you realise the mistake, and contact your insurer or roadside assistance straight away.

If my engine catches fire, is that covered?

Fire is usually an insured event, but if the fire was caused by an excluded mechanical failure, the claim can still be rejected. In one ombud case, a fire caused by a snapped conrod leaking oil onto a hot component was found to be excluded because it flowed directly from mechanical breakdown.

What can I do if my engine damage claim is rejected?

Ask for the reasons and the assessor’s report in writing, gather your service records and any independent mechanical report, make written representations to the insurer within the time in your rejection letter, and if you’re still unhappy, complain to the National Financial Ombud’s Non-life Division for free.
