How to claim on your car insurance in South Africa, step by step

A car insurance claim starts at the scene, not on the claim form. What you do in the first hour and the first working day shapes how smoothly it goes. Here are the steps, what the law requires, and the timelines your insurer has to keep to.

Quick answer Stop, help anyone injured, and exchange names, addresses and registration numbers. If the accident isn’t reported to a police or traffic officer at the scene, report it at a police station or traffic office within 24 hours if anyone was hurt, or on the first working day if no one was. Take photographs, then notify your insurer or broker as soon as possible with the case number and your documents. The insurer must decide within a reasonable period, tell you in writing within 10 days of deciding, and, if it rejects the claim, give reasons and at least 90 days for you to respond.

At the scene: what the law requires

Section 61 of the National Road Traffic Act sets out what every driver involved in an accident must do. In summary, you must (National Road Traffic Act, section 61):

  1. Stop the vehicle.
  2. Check whether anyone is injured and help them as far as you reasonably can.
  3. Check the damage to vehicles and property.
  4. Give your name and address, the vehicle owner’s name and address, and the registration number to anyone with reasonable grounds to ask.
  5. Report the accident if it wasn’t reported to a police or traffic officer at the scene (see below).
  6. Don’t drink alcohol or take drugs until you have reported the accident, if anyone was killed or injured, unless a doctor prescribes it.

Reporting to the police: 24 hours or the first working day

The deadline depends on whether anyone was hurt. If the accident wasn’t reported to a police or traffic officer at the scene, section 61(1)(f) requires you to report it at a police station or traffic office:

SituationReport within
Someone was killed or injured24 hours
Damage only, no one hurtThe first working day after the accident

Take your driving licence and ID number with you. The police will give you a case or accident report number. Insurers commonly ask for it, so keep it with your claim papers.

Gather evidence before you leave

Photographs and details taken on the day are hard to replace later. If it’s safe, record:

  • Photographs of all vehicles from several angles, the number plates, the road, skid marks, signs and traffic lights
  • The other driver’s name, ID number, contact details, licence details and insurer, if they’ll share it
  • The registration, make, model and colour of every vehicle involved
  • Names and numbers of witnesses
  • The date, time, exact location and weather
  • A short note of what happened, written while you remember it
Careful what you say. Be polite, but avoid admitting fault or agreeing to settle privately at the scene. Many policies require you not to admit liability without the insurer’s consent. Check your wording.

Notify your insurer or broker

Tell your insurer as soon as you can, within the time limit in your policy. The Policyholder Protection Rules require insurers to tell you what information they need, where and how to submit a claim, the time limits, and any excess or admin fees that apply (Policyholder Protection Rules, rule 17).

If your broker is mandated by the insurer to manage claims, a claim the broker receives counts as received by the insurer. The insurer must then acknowledge your claim within a reasonable time, with contact details, indicative timelines and a list of anything still outstanding.

Report the claim even if you think the other driver is to blame. Your insurer may pay for your repairs and then recover its costs from the other party.

Assessment, repairs and the excess

After you submit, the insurer usually arranges an assessment of the damage. Depending on your policy, an assessor may inspect the car, or you may be asked for quotes. The insurer then authorises repairs, often at an approved repairer, or settles the claim. If repair would cost more than the car is worth, it may be written off and settled at the value set out in your policy.

You usually pay the excess, either to the repairer or by deduction from the payout. The rules say an excess must be disclosed, fair and reasonable, and not an unreasonable barrier to claiming. Our car insurance excess guide explains how excesses are set.

The insurer’s decision: timelines and your rights

The Policyholder Protection Rules set clear limits on how insurers handle a decision. Under rule 17:

StageWhat the rules require
DecidingWithin a reasonable period, after a reasonable investigation
Telling youIn writing, within 10 days of the decision
If rejectedReasons, at least 90 days for you to make representations, the internal escalation process and the ombud’s details
Your representationsThe insurer must decide on them within 45 days
Time bar for legal actionCan’t include the 90 days, and must allow at least 6 months after them

Insurers also may not discourage you from using an attorney or assessor, and may not reject a claim based only on a polygraph test. If you disagree with a rejection, our guide to rejected claims and the National Financial Ombud walks through the next steps.

If someone was injured

Car insurance mainly deals with damage to vehicles and property. Claims for injury or death caused by a road accident in South Africa usually go to the Road Accident Fund, subject to its rules (Road Accident Fund Act, section 17). Report the accident to the police within 24 hours and keep the case number and medical records.

Key takeaways

  • Stop, help the injured and exchange details. Section 61 of the National Road Traffic Act requires it.
  • Report to the police within 24 hours if anyone was hurt, or on the first working day if not, unless an officer recorded it at the scene.
  • Photograph everything and don’t admit fault at the scene.
  • Notify your insurer or broker quickly, within your policy’s time limit.
  • The insurer must decide within a reasonable period, notify you within 10 days of deciding, and give you at least 90 days to challenge a rejection.

Car insurance claim FAQs

General answers about claiming on car insurance in South Africa. Your policy wording sets the specific rules for your claim.

How long do I have to report a car accident to the police?

Under section 61 of the National Road Traffic Act, if the accident wasn’t reported to a police or traffic officer at the scene, you must report it at a police station or traffic office within 24 hours if anyone was killed or injured. If no one was hurt, report it on the first working day after the accident.

How soon must I tell my insurer about a claim?

Your policy sets the deadline, so check your wording. Many policies ask you to notify the insurer as soon as reasonably possible. Insurers must tell you their time limits for claims.

What documents do I need for a car insurance claim?

Insurers commonly ask for a completed claim form, your driving licence, the police case or accident report number, photographs, the other driver’s details, witness details, and repair quotes if requested. Your insurer must tell you what information it needs and how to submit it.

Do I pay the excess if the accident wasn’t my fault?

It depends on your policy. Many insurers deduct the excess from the claim and may try to recover it, along with their own costs, from the other driver or their insurer. Ask your insurer how it handles excess when the other party is at fault.

How long does an insurer have to decide on my claim?

The Policyholder Protection Rules require the insurer to decide within a reasonable period and to tell you in writing within 10 days of the decision. If it rejects the claim, it must give reasons and allow at least 90 days for you to make representations.

What can I do if my car insurance claim is rejected?

You have at least 90 days to ask the insurer to reconsider. The insurer must decide on your representations within 45 days. You can also escalate through its internal complaints process and then to the National Financial Ombud Scheme.

Does car insurance pay for injuries in an accident?

Car insurance mainly deals with damage to vehicles and property. Claims for injury or death in road accidents in South Africa usually go through the Road Accident Fund, subject to its rules.
