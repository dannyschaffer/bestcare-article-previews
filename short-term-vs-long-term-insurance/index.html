Short-term vs long-term insurance: what’s the difference?

South Africans still say short-term and long-term, but the law now calls them non-life and life insurance. Here’s what separates the two, where common products sit, and why it matters when you buy, cancel or complain.

Quick answer Short-term (non-life) insurance covers loss or damage to things and liability to other people, such as your car, home, contents and business. It pays to put right an actual loss. Long-term (life) insurance pays benefits linked to a person’s life, health, disability or death, such as life cover, funeral cover and income protection. The Insurance Act 18 of 2017 defines the two as non-life and life insurance policies (Insurance Act, section 1).

The core difference: things vs people

Short-term insurance compensates you for a loss. Long-term insurance pays a benefit when a defined event happens to a person. The Insurance Act draws the line this way:

  • Non-life (short-term): an arrangement under which the insurer “fully or partially indemnifies loss on the happening of an unplanned or uncertain event”, other than a life event, or a death or disability that isn’t caused by an accident.
  • Life (long-term): an arrangement under which the insurer meets obligations on a life, health, disability or death event, or on or from a fixed date.

Where common products sit

Most products fall clearly on one side, but some that sound like “life” products are legally short-term. Schedule 2 of the Insurance Act lists the classes of each:

The accident and health class is why personal accident cover, which pays if someone dies or is disabled in an accident, can be short-term insurance. Travel insurance is short-term too. Health insurance products that overlap with medical schemes are governed by separate demarcation rules. The Council for Medical Schemes has extended the current exemption framework to 31 March 2027 (CMS Circular 9 of 2025).

Test yourself: short-term or long-term?

Four products. Pick the side each one sits on under the Insurance Act.

1. A policy that pays out to cover funeral costs when a family member dies.
2. Cover for medical emergencies and lost luggage on an overseas trip.
3. Cover that pays employees a benefit if they’re disabled in an accident at work.
4. A policy that settles your home loan if you die before it’s paid off.

Why the difference matters to you

The two types sit under different rules for cancelling, complaining and getting advice.

Cooling-off periods differ

Under the Policyholder Protection Rules, you can cancel a new short-term policy with a term longer than 31 days within 14 days of receiving the policy, if nothing has been claimed (Short-term PPR 4.2). For long-term policies the period is 31 days (Long-term PPR 4.2). Premiums paid are refunded, less the cost of any cover you actually had (and, for long-term policies with an investment element, any market loss) (PPR 4.3).

Complaints go to different divisions

The National Financial Ombud Scheme started on 1 March 2024. It replaced the separate short-term, long-term, banking and credit ombuds, and it has a Non-life Insurance division and a Life Insurance division (NFO). Complaints about the advice you received from a financial services provider go to the FAIS Ombud. Our guide to escalating a rejected claim explains the process.

Advisers are licensed separately

Financial advisers and brokers are authorised for specific product categories, so a short-term broker isn’t automatically licensed to advise on life cover, and the reverse. You can check an adviser’s categories on the FSCA’s register of financial services providers.

Both sectors are regulated by the Prudential Authority for financial soundness and the Financial Sector Conduct Authority for how customers are treated. The two differ in scale: life insurers managed R5.2 trillion in assets at the end of 2025 and paid R626 billion in claims and benefits during the year (ASISA, March 2026).

Key takeaways

  • Short-term (non-life) insurance indemnifies loss to property and liability; long-term (life) pays benefits linked to a person.
  • The Insurance Act now uses the terms non-life and life.
  • Personal accident and travel cover are short-term classes; funeral and credit life are long-term.
  • Cooling-off: 14 days for new short-term policies, 31 days for long-term.
  • The NFO handles complaints about both, in separate divisions.

Short-term vs long-term insurance FAQs

General answers about how insurance is classified in South Africa.

What is the difference between short-term and long-term insurance?

Short-term (non-life) insurance indemnifies loss or damage to property and liability to others, such as car, home and business cover. Long-term (life) insurance pays benefits on events linked to a person, such as death, disability or illness, or on a fixed date.

Is funeral cover short-term or long-term insurance?

Long-term. Funeral is one of the life insurance classes in Schedule 2 of the Insurance Act.

Is personal accident cover short-term insurance?

It can be. The non-life accident and health class covers death or disability caused by an accident, so personal accident cover can be written as short-term insurance.

Why is it called short-term insurance?

Short-term policies are usually renewed monthly or annually, with the cover and premium reviewed each time. The older Short-term and Long-term Insurance Acts used these names, and the Insurance Act 18 of 2017 now uses non-life and life.

How long is the cooling-off period on a new policy?

Under the Policyholder Protection Rules, 14 days after receiving the policy for a short-term policy with a term longer than 31 days, and 31 days for a long-term policy, provided nothing has been claimed.

Where do I complain about a short-term or life insurer?

The National Financial Ombud Scheme handles both, through its Non-life Insurance and Life Insurance divisions. Complaints about advice from a financial services provider go to the FAIS Ombud.
