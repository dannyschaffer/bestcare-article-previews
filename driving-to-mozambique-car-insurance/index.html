Driving to Mozambique this December: the insurance you need at the border, and what your South African policy may not do

Thousands of South African families drive to Mozambique over the holidays. Two kinds of insurance matter on that trip: the Mozambican third-party cover you must have at the border, and your own car insurance, which may or may not follow you across it. Here’s what to sort out before you pack.

Quick answer Mozambique requires third-party insurance for foreign vehicles. You can buy it at most land borders, and the certificate is valid for 30 days from issue, with one needed for the car and one for each towed trailer or boat. You’ll also need a temporary import permit, completed at the border, plus two reflective triangles and two reflective vests. Separately, check your South African motor policy’s territorial limits: whether it covers damage to and theft of your own car in Mozambique, for how many days, and on what conditions. The Road Accident Fund only covers accidents in South Africa.

Mozambican third-party insurance is compulsory

Every foreign vehicle needs Mozambican third-party liability insurance. The UK government’s travel advice puts it plainly: you are legally required to have third-party insurance, which you can buy at most land borders (GOV.UK, Mozambique travel advice).

The UN World Food Programme’s logistics assessment of the Ressano Garcia crossing, the main border on the N4 from Gauteng, gives more detail. It lists a compulsory third-party liability certificate, valid for 30 days from the date issued and obtainable at the border, needed per vehicle and per towed trailer or boat (Logistics Cluster, Ressano Garcia).

Third-party cover pays for injury to other people or damage to their property that you cause. It usually doesn’t cover your own car. That’s the job of your own motor policy, if it extends to Mozambique.

Staying longer than 30 days? The certificate described at Ressano Garcia runs for 30 days from issue. If your trip is longer, find out before you go how to renew it in Mozambique.

What your South African car insurance may not do in Mozambique

Your own policy’s territorial limits decide whether damage to, or theft of, your car is covered across the border. Some South African motor policies extend to certain neighbouring countries, sometimes only for a set number of days or with extra conditions. Others don’t. Don’t assume, and don’t rely on what applied on a previous trip, because policies change at renewal.

Before you travel, ask your insurer or broker:

  • Does my policy cover my car in Mozambique, and for how many days in a row?
  • Are theft and hijacking covered there on the same terms as at home?
  • Does the cover include the trailer, caravan or boat I’m towing?
  • Is roadside assistance or towing available in Mozambique, and how would a damaged car be brought home?
  • Is my liability to other people covered there, or only the Mozambican third-party certificate?
  • Do I need a letter from you confirming cover, for the border or for the trip?

Ask for the answers in writing and keep them with your policy schedule. If you’re towing, our caravan and trailer cover guide and watercraft guide list the questions specific to those.

The Road Accident Fund stops at the border

At home, injuries to other people in a crash are mostly dealt with by the Road Accident Fund. In Mozambique, they aren’t. The Road Accident Fund Act covers injury or death caused by the driving of a motor vehicle “at any place within the Republic” (Road Accident Fund Act, section 17). That’s one reason Mozambique requires its own third-party cover, and why it’s worth knowing what your policy does there.

On the road in Mozambique

Police checks are frequent, and missing documents or safety kit can mean an on-the-spot fine. The GOV.UK advice says traffic police may fine you if you can’t show your licence, ownership and insurance documents, or the two triangles and two vests. If you’re fined for no clear reason, it suggests asking for a written fine you can pay at a police station (GOV.UK).

The same advice recommends travelling by road outside major cities only in daylight, keeping to major roads, and checking conditions first, because low-lying areas around major rivers flood regularly in the rainy season. December falls in that season. Fuel supplies can also be unpredictable, so plan your stops.

If you are in an accident, the steps for gathering details and photographs are much the same as at home. Our step-by-step car claim guide covers what your insurer will usually ask for. Contact your insurer as soon as you can, even from abroad.

Key takeaways

  • Mozambican third-party insurance is compulsory for foreign vehicles and can be bought at most land borders.
  • The certificate described at Ressano Garcia is valid for 30 days, per vehicle and per towed trailer or boat.
  • Carry two reflective triangles and two reflective vests, and complete a temporary import permit at the border.
  • Your own policy’s territorial limits decide whether your car is covered in Mozambique. Get confirmation in writing.
  • The Road Accident Fund only covers accidents within South Africa.

Driving to Mozambique FAQs

General answers for South Africans driving to Mozambique. Your policy schedule and wording decide what your own cover does.

Do I need third-party insurance to drive in Mozambique?

Yes. The UK government’s travel advice says you are legally required to have third-party insurance in Mozambique and that it can be bought at most land borders. A UN logistics assessment of the Ressano Garcia crossing describes the certificate as compulsory and valid for 30 days from the date issued.

Does my South African car insurance cover me in Mozambique?

It depends on your policy’s territorial limits. Some South African motor policies extend to certain neighbouring countries, sometimes for a limited number of days or with conditions. Check your schedule and ask your insurer to confirm in writing before you travel.

Do I need separate third-party insurance for my trailer or boat?

The UN logistics assessment of the Ressano Garcia border lists the compulsory third-party certificate per vehicle and per towed trailer or boat. Plan for a certificate for each, and check your own policy covers the trailer or boat outside South Africa.

What safety equipment must I carry in Mozambique?

Carry two red reflective warning triangles and two reflective vests. The UK government’s advice says you can be fined on the spot if you can’t produce them.

What documents do I need to drive my car into Mozambique?

Commonly listed documents include your passport, driving licence, vehicle registration papers, a temporary import permit completed at the border, and the Mozambican third-party insurance certificate. If the car is financed, ask your lender what written permission it requires.

Does the Road Accident Fund cover accidents in Mozambique?

No. The Road Accident Fund Act covers injury or death caused by driving a motor vehicle within South Africa. For accidents in Mozambique, third-party claims are a matter for Mozambican cover and your own policy terms.

Can I buy Mozambican third-party insurance before I leave?

Some providers sell it in advance, and it can also be bought at most land borders. Buying ahead can save time in December queues; keep the certificate with your vehicle documents.
