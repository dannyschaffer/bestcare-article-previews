Types of short-term insurance in South Africa, explained

The main types of short-term insurance in South Africa protect your car, your home, your belongings and your legal liability to other people, with a longer list for businesses. Here’s how the law groups them, what each type is for, with examples, and where to read more about each one.

Quick answer Short-term insurance, now called non-life insurance in the law, pays out when something you own is damaged, lost or stolen, or when you’re legally liable for harm to someone else. The Insurance Act 18 of 2017 sets out 17 classes, from motor and property to liability, engineering and travel (Insurance Act, Schedule 2, Table 2). For most households the main types are car, buildings, home contents, portable possessions and personal liability cover. Yet only 11% of South African adults held any non-life insurance in 2023 (FinScope Consumer South Africa (2023)).

What counts as short-term insurance

Short-term insurance covers things and liabilities rather than lives: it indemnifies you, meaning it puts you back in roughly the financial position you were in, after a sudden event such as a crash, a burglary, a fire or a hailstorm. The Insurance Act defines a non-life policy as one that “fully or partially indemnifies loss on the happening of an unplanned or uncertain event”, other than life events and non-accidental death or disability (Insurance Act 18 of 2017, s1).

The name changed in 2018. The Insurance Act (2017) took effect on 1 July 2018 and replaced parts of the Short-term Insurance Act of 1998, but “short-term insurance” is still the term most people, brokers and many insurers use. The policyholder rules you rely on at claim time, the Policyholder Protection Rules (Short-term Insurance), still sit under the older Act. Life cover, funeral policies and retirement annuities are long-term insurance, a separate category explained in our guide to short-term vs long-term insurance.

The law also splits the market two ways. Personal lines is cover bought by a person “acting otherwise than solely for the purposes of the person’s own business”, and commercial lines is everything else. That split, plus the 17 classes below, is the official map of every type of short-term insurance sold in South Africa.

Types of short-term insurance for individuals and families

For a household, short-term insurance usually comes down to five building blocks: the car, the house, what’s inside it, the valuables you carry, and your liability to other people. The FSCA’s consumer guide describes the core ones in a line each: motor insurance “covers your motor vehicle against collision, fire and theft”, household contents cover “furniture, TV, radio and other valuables in your house”, and house owners cover the “bricks, roof, fittings and other things that are needed to rebuild your house” (FSCA, Know more about short-term insurance).

Car and vehicle insurance

Motor cover usually comes in three levels. Comprehensive car insurance commonly includes accident damage to your own car as well as theft, fire and damage you cause to others. Third party, fire and theft drops accident damage to your own car. Third-party-only cover deals only with the damage you cause to other people’s property. What “third party” means in practice is explained in our guide to third-party claims. The same motor class includes motorcycle insurance and cover for a caravan or trailer.

Before you choose a level, it helps to understand retail, market and trade value, how car insurance premiums are calculated and how the excess works. If you drive for an e-hailing platform or use a bakkie for work, the way you use the vehicle matters too: see e-hailing insurance and bakkie business use.

Buildings insurance

Buildings insurance covers the structure: walls, roof, fixed fittings, and often the geyser and outbuildings. If you have a home loan, your bank will normally require it. Our buildings insurance guide covers what it includes, and sectional title insurance explains how a body corporate policy changes things for flat and townhouse owners.

Home contents insurance

Home contents insurance covers what you’d take with you if you moved: furniture, appliances, clothing and electronics. It’s the type tenants usually need, because a landlord’s buildings policy normally covers the building, not a tenant’s belongings. Read what contents insurance costs and buildings vs contents.

Portable possessions (all risks)

Portable possessions cover, often called “all risks”, extends to items you carry away from home, such as phones, laptops, cameras and jewellery. Contents cover usually stops at your front door, so items you take out with you often need to be listed here.

Personal liability and other personal cover

Personal liability insurance pays when you’re legally liable for injury or damage to someone outside your household, for example if your dog bites a visitor or your tree falls on the neighbour’s car. The FSCA describes it as cover for “damages to a person or property that is not part of your household” (FSCA). Other personal types include watercraft, travel and personal cyber insurance.

Businesses face all of these risks and a set of their own, which is where the list gets longer.

Types of short-term insurance for businesses

Business short-term insurance protects three things: the business’s property, its income when it can’t trade, and its liability to customers, the public and employees. Most small businesses start with a business insurance package that combines several sections, then add specialist cover as the risks grow.

There’s one more type that sits on top of both personal and business policies, and it often confuses people.

Sasria: special risks cover that sits on top

Sasria cover protects against damage from riots, strikes, public unrest and terrorism, which ordinary short-term policies usually exclude. Sasria is state-owned and describes its cover as protecting “your valuables damaged during unforeseen public unrest, civil commotion, strikes, riots and terrorism” (Sasria).

You don’t buy Sasria cover on its own. It’s added to an existing motor, property or business policy through your insurer or broker, and Sasria’s own policy documents say that to be covered “you must have an underlying policy contract in force that includes Sasria cover at the time of the event” (Sasria, Business Interruption (2026)). It’s usually a small amount on the schedule, so it’s worth checking it’s there.

How many South Africans have short-term insurance?

Not many. FinScope’s 2023 consumer survey found that “only 11% of individuals, or about 5 million, possessed non-life insurance”, compared with 48% who held a funeral policy (FinMark Trust, FinScope Consumer South Africa (2023)). On the roads, the AA has reported a SAIA estimate that “about 65% of South African motorists are not insured” (AA of South Africa (2023)), which matters for anyone hit by another driver.

The industry is still large. The Prudential Authority reports that non-life primary insurers, cell captives and captives wrote R237.5 billion in gross premiums in the 12 months to 31 December 2025, up from R224.9 billion a year earlier (Prudential Authority Annual Report 2025/26, Table 15). Not having cover usually comes down to cost or not knowing which type fits, and the second is the easier one to fix.

Key takeaways

  • Short-term insurance (non-life insurance in the law) covers property and liability against sudden, unplanned events.
  • The Insurance Act 18 of 2017 sets out 17 classes, from motor and property to liability, transport and travel.
  • Most households need some mix of car, buildings, home contents, portable possessions and personal liability cover.
  • Businesses add property, business interruption, liability, cyber, transit and specialist covers.

Short-term insurance FAQs

General answers about the types of short-term insurance available in South Africa.

What are the main types of short-term insurance?

For individuals, the main types are car (motor) insurance, buildings insurance, home contents insurance, portable possessions or all risks cover, and personal liability insurance, with travel and watercraft cover as extras. Businesses add commercial property, business interruption, public and products liability, professional indemnity, cyber, goods in transit and specialist covers.

What are examples of short-term insurance?

Common examples are comprehensive car insurance, buildings insurance on a house, home contents insurance, all risks cover on a phone or laptop, personal liability cover, travel insurance, and for businesses public liability, business interruption and goods in transit insurance.

How many classes of short-term insurance are there in South Africa?

Table 2 of Schedule 2 to the Insurance Act 18 of 2017 lists 17 classes of non-life insurance: motor, property, agriculture, engineering, marine, aviation, transport, rail, legal expense, liability, consumer credit, trade credit, guarantee, accident and health, travel, miscellaneous and reinsurance.

Is short-term insurance the same as non-life insurance?

Yes. Since the Insurance Act took effect on 1 July 2018, the law uses the term non-life insurance. Short-term insurance is the older name from the Short-term Insurance Act of 1998 and is still widely used.

What is the difference between short-term and long-term insurance?

Short-term insurance covers property and liability against sudden events such as accidents, theft, fire or storms, and is usually renewed monthly or yearly. Long-term insurance covers life events such as death, disability and retirement, for example life cover and funeral policies.

What is the difference between personal lines and commercial lines?

Personal lines is non-life insurance bought by a person who isn’t acting solely for their own business, such as a household policy. Commercial lines is all other non-life insurance, mainly cover bought by businesses.

Is car insurance compulsory in South Africa?

No. Vehicle insurance isn’t a legal requirement in South Africa, although a bank or finance house will normally require comprehensive cover on a financed car. The Road Accident Fund covers injury and death claims, not damage to vehicles or property.

What is Sasria cover?

Sasria cover protects against damage caused by riots, strikes, public unrest, civil commotion and terrorism, which ordinary policies usually exclude. It’s added to an existing motor, property or business policy, not bought on its own.
